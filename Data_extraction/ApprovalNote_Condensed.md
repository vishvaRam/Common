## Definition and Classification Guidelines for CII
Critical Information Infrastructure (CII) is defined by the Information Technology Act 2000 as computer resources whose incapacitation would severely impact national security, economy, public health, or safety. The perimeter of CII includes internet web servers, NIPS, NIDS, external routers, network/application/database firewalls, proxy, and Internet DNS servers. NCIIPC guidelines from 2019 for identifying CII focus on factors such as: size and economic value (transaction value/volume, network size), criticality of business processes and estimated impact magnitude (on national security, economy, public health, safety, customers, business, government), timeframe for significant impact (shorter timeframe indicates higher criticality), and level of dependency (impact of non-availability and dependence on other critical sectors).

Additionally, RBI’s Advisory No.xx/20xx dated 02.06.2025 on "Analysis of Incidents reported and Emerging Threats – Protection of Critical Information Systems (CIS)" introduced further parameters for CII classification. These include Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability (CIA) of data, storage of sensitive PII data, storage of card data, Business Impact Analysis (BIA), interdependencies, and Recovery Objectives.

- CII definition from Information Technology Act 2000: incapacitation leads to debilitating impact on national security, economy, public health or safety.
- NCIIPC guidelines (2019) criteria: Size and Economic Value, Criticality and Impact Magnitude, Timeframe for Impact, Level of Dependency.
- RBI's Advisory No.xx/20xx (02.06.2025) introduced additional parameters: CIA of data, PII data storage, card data storage, BIA, interdependencies, Recovery Objectives.
